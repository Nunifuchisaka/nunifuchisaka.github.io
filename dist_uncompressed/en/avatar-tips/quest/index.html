Avatar Tips #5

Quest and mobile support

People on Quest or phones can't see avatars made for PC. To be seen by them, you upload a separate, lighter version (an Android version and an iOS version) to the same avatar. This tip explains why you need a separate version, what you can't use, and how to make one.

Read this when

  • A friend on Quest says your avatar looks like a blurry figure or a robot.
  • You edited an avatar for PC and want people on Quest or phones to see it too.
  • You uploaded a Quest version, but people say they still can't see it.

Why you need a separate version

VRChat runs on PC (Windows), Android (Quest and phones) and iOS (iPhone and iPad). You build and upload your avatar separately for each one. Quest and phones are much less powerful than a PC, so heavy content and some components can't be used.

When you upload, you put the PC version and the Android version side by side in the same avatar (the same Blueprint ID). Each viewer automatically gets the version that matches their device.

What people on Quest see

There is an Android version
They see your Android avatar. But if its rank is low, they may not see it (see "How strict the ranks are" below).
There is only a PC version
They see an impostor that VRChat made automatically (a blurry stand-in built from flat panels). If they turned impostors off, they see the fallback avatar they chose instead (such as a robot).
About impostors
You can also regenerate the impostor on your avatar's page on the VRChat website. Only humanoid avatars are supported. It's only a stand-in, so expressions and outfit toggles don't come through.

What you can't use on Quest and mobile

Quest and mobile avatars can't use the things below. If your avatar has them, they get removed or simply don't work.

  • Only the "VRChat/Mobile" shaders that VRChat provides are allowed. Shaders like lilToon can't be used.
  • Lights, cameras, Cloth, sound (Audio Source), and physics components like Rigidbody and Collider don't work at all.
  • Unity Constraints don't work, so use VRChat Constraints. Dynamic Bone doesn't work either, so use PhysBone.
  • Particles are heavily restricted.
  • An Android avatar can't be uploaded if it's over 10MB after compression. Aim for about 5 to 8MB.

Transparency (like soft, faded hair tips) isn't available, so make things opaque or use a sharp cutout. Turn on "Enable GPU Instancing" on your materials.

  1. In the Shader list, choose "VRChat/Mobile" and pick a shader inside it. If you leave another shader, the avatar shows up pink or invisible on Quest.
  2. Turn this on to make it lighter.

How strict the ranks are

On Quest and mobile, the performance rank limits are much stricter than on PC. On top of that, by default, people on Quest and mobile can only see avatars up to Medium. Poor and Very Poor avatars turn into an impostor or a fallback.

Viewers can change a setting to see avatars up to Poor, but they can't choose to see Very Poor ones (they can still select one person and press "Show Avatar" to see them one at a time). Aim for Medium or better on your Quest version.

First, check the store page

Many avatars and outfits say "Quest compatible" (Quest 対応) on their store page, and come with Quest data. If yours does, using it is the quickest way. Place the Quest prefab and edit it the same way you did for PC.

If there's no Quest data, you lighten the avatar yourself. Every outfit or accessory you add makes it heavier, so a simple look is a good choice for your Quest version.

Steps to make a Quest version

  1. Check that "Android Build Support" is installed for your Unity. If it isn't, add it in Unity Hub (see #1 Installing Unity with Unity Hub).
  2. Duplicate your PC avatar and use the copy for Quest. You don't touch the original, so your PC version stays as it is.
  3. Open the VRChat SDK Control Panel (VRChat SDK → Show Control Panel), and in the "Builder" tab, switch Platform to "Android". If a confirmation appears, continue.
  4. Change every material on the Quest avatar to a "VRChat/Mobile" shader. Keeping separate materials means you won't break the look of the PC version.
  5. Delete the components you can't use (lights, Cloth, sound, Unity Constraints and so on). Also read the warnings and errors in the SDK Control Panel.
  6. Reduce polygons, materials and PhysBones until the rank is Medium or better.
  7. Upload to the same Blueprint ID as the PC version. If you duplicated the avatar in the same project, the Pipeline Manager already has that ID.
  8. Check it on your own Quest, or ask someone on Quest to look.
  1. Choose where to upload. Everything you check is uploaded in one go. If you pick a platform other than the one that's open now, you're asked to confirm the switch.
  2. Press this to build and upload for each platform you chose. The first switch to Android reimports everything, so it takes a while.

Tools that save effort

  • VRChat's docs introduce "EasyQuestSwitch", which switches settings between the PC and Quest versions. You can add it from the VCC.
  • There is also "VRCQuestTools", which duplicates your PC avatar, makes Quest materials and textures, and removes components you can't use. But it doesn't lighten the avatar enough for the rank, so as-is the result often ends up Very Poor.
  • If you made separate avatars for PC and Quest, the SDK's "Per-Platform Override" lets you upload them together as one avatar.

What to keep the same on PC and Quest

  • Keep the bone structure (Armature) the same. If you change the names or order of key bones like the head, hands and feet, or the size and direction of Hips, your avatar moves strangely when seen from other devices. Removing hair or skirt bones is fine.
  • It's also safer to keep the Expressions menu and parameters the same. If an outfit toggle exists on only one version, people on the other version won't see it change.

iOS (iPhone and iPad)

VRChat also runs on iOS. Making an iOS version is almost the same as making an Android one, with the same restrictions and limits. To make one, add "iOS Build Support" in Unity Hub, choose "iOS" in Platform, and upload.

If your avatar has no iOS version, people on iOS see it as an impostor.

Common problems

It turns pink

What happens
People on Quest say part or all of your avatar looks pink.
Cause
The Quest version still has materials using shaders other than VRChat/Mobile.
How to fix it
Go through the materials on the Quest avatar one by one and change them to VRChat/Mobile shaders. Don't miss the materials on outfits and accessories.

It looks blurry or like a robot

What happens
People on Quest say you look like a blurry figure (an impostor) or a robot.
Cause
No Android version was uploaded, or it was uploaded to a different avatar (a different Blueprint ID) from the PC version.
How to fix it
Set Platform to Android and upload to the same Blueprint ID as the PC version. Check your avatar's details in VRChat to see whether it has an Android version.

There's a Quest version, but they can't see it

What happens
You uploaded an Android version, but people on Quest still can't see it.
Cause
Its rank is Poor or Very Poor. By default, people on Quest and mobile can only see avatars up to Medium.
How to fix it
Reduce polygons, materials, PhysBones and so on until it's Medium or better. Check the Android rank in the SDK Control Panel.

Switching takes a very long time

What happens
When you switch Platform to Android, you have to wait for tens of minutes.
Cause
The first switch reimports everything, including textures.
How to fix it
Just wait the first time. It's faster after that. Don't close Unity while it's switching.

You only updated one version

What happens
You edited and uploaded only the PC version, and people on Quest still see your old look.
Cause
You didn't upload the Android version again. Each version is stored separately.
How to fix it
Make the same edits to the Quest version and upload it again. If you check both Windows and Android in Platform, you can upload them together.

Things to watch out for

  • VRChat has said that Very Poor Quest and mobile avatars may stop being usable in the future. Don't let your Quest version be Very Poor.
  • Check your Quest version on your own Quest, or ask a friend on Quest to look. Unity on your PC can't show you how it looks on Quest.
  • Follow the terms of use of your avatar and outfits when you make a Quest version of your edits, too. Also check that they allow editing and uploading a Quest version.

Summary

  • To be seen by people on Quest and phones, upload an Android (and iOS) version to the same Blueprint ID as the PC version.
  • Only VRChat/Mobile shaders are allowed. Lights, Cloth, sound and similar components can't be used.
  • By default, people on Quest and mobile can only see avatars up to Medium, so aim for Medium or better.
  • Start by checking the store page to see if Quest data is included.
