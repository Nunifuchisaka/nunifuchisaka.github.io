Render Queue (draw order)

See-through hair, lace or glasses lenses that vanish at some angles, or slip behind other things. Most of the time, a number called the Render Queue — the order things are drawn in — is the fix.

When to read this

  • A see-through part (lace, a glasses lens, blush) disappears and reappears as you move the camera.
  • In worlds with water, see-through parts of your avatar get hidden by the water surface.
  • You can't see one see-through part through another, such as a ribbon behind a sheer skirt.
  • Particles and effects jump in front of or behind your hair and clothes.

What the Render Queue is

When Unity draws the screen, it draws each material in order of a number, smallest first. That number is the Render Queue. Things drawn later are laid over things drawn earlier.

Solid (opaque) things decide what is in front by depth, so changing their order barely changes how they look. The order matters for see-through (transparent) things, which don't record depth and simply blend their color over whatever is already on screen.

Above 2500, the sorting changes

2500 and below (solid)
Drawn roughly nearest first. Depth decides what is in front, so the result looks right either way.
2501 and above (see-through)
Among materials with the same number, the one farthest from the camera is drawn first. The distance is measured to the center of each mesh's bounds, not to the surface you see. A big skirt and a small ribbon have centers in different places, so as the camera moves, which one counts as nearer can flip, and the part flickers.
Several see-through materials on one mesh
Within one mesh with the same number, they are drawn in the order of the material slots (Element 0, 1, …), not by distance.

Common problems and fixes

It comes and goes with the angle

What you see
Two see-through parts swap which one is in front as you look around.
Why
They have the same number, so the order is decided by distance, and the distance check flips with the angle.
Fix
Give the part that should be in front a slightly larger number (+1 to +10). The order is then fixed by the number, not the distance.

It hides behind water

What you see
In worlds with water, see-through parts of your avatar look like they are under the surface.
Why
Water surfaces often use numbers in the see-through range. If your part is drawn before the water, the water gets painted over it.
Fix
Set your part's number above the water's. My SpiderWeb uses 3100 for the threads and 3110 for the shockwave; in worlds whose water is 3100 or higher, its manual says to raise the thread material above the water and the shockwave above that. SpiderWeb manual

Inside one mesh

What you see
Two see-through materials on the same mesh overlap in the wrong order.
Why
With the same number, they are drawn in the order of the material slots.
Fix
Give the material that should be in front a larger number, or put the one behind in an earlier slot.

When the edge can be sharp, use Cutout

What you see
Hair tips or lace holes are see-through only to cut out shapes.
Why
See-through materials need ordering; Cutout (2450) is treated as solid and records depth.
Fix
If a crisp edge is fine, switch to Cutout. It doesn't depend on order at all.

How to change the number

  1. Duplicate the material first, so you can go back easily.
  2. Select the material in the Project window.
  3. Find the Render Queue field in the Inspector. In many shaders it is near the bottom. With From Shader, the shader's default is used.
  4. Type the number in.
  5. To check the order, open Unity's Frame Debugger (Window → Analysis → Frame Debugger) and step through what is drawn, one call at a time.

Watch out for

  • Above 2500, a material is treated as see-through. In the Built-in Render Pipeline, things above 2500 no longer receive shadows. Keep solid things at 2500 or below.
  • Changing a shader's rendering mode can reset the Render Queue to the default. Check the number again afterwards.
  • Move it only a little. The larger the number, the later it is drawn than the world's see-through things (glass, fog, effects), and it can look like it is in front of them even when it is behind.
  • The Render Queue is a material setting, so once uploaded, other players see the same order.

Summary

  • The Render Queue is the order things are drawn in. It matters for see-through materials.
  • With the same number, distance to the center of the mesh decides the order, and that is what makes things flicker.
  • Fix the order you want with small differences in the number, and use Cutout where you can.
