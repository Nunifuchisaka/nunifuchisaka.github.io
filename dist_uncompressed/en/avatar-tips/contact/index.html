Avatar Tips #11

Gimmicks that react to touch

Your cheeks turn red when someone pats your head. Your face looks surprised when someone grabs your tail. VRChat's Contacts and PhysBone parameters tell the Animator that you were touched. This tip explains how they work, how to build them yourself, and how they look to other people.

Read this when

  • You want your avatar to react with an expression or a blush when someone pats your head.
  • You want your expression to change when someone grabs your hair or tail.
  • Nothing happens when someone touches you. Or it reacts to your own hands too.
  • You can see the reaction yourself, but you don't know whether other people can see it.

How Contacts work

A Contact is a VRChat component for detecting a "touch". It's separate from Unity's normal collision (Colliders). It puts whether something is touching into a parameter, like the ones you saw in #6 How the Animator works.

VRC Contact Sender (the sending side)
A component that only says "I'm here". VRChat automatically adds Senders to the head, body, hands, feet and fingers of humanoid avatars.
VRC Contact Receiver (the receiving side)
A component that puts a value into an Animator parameter when a Sender enters it. You place it yourself where you want a reaction.
Collision Tags
It only reacts when the Sender and the Receiver share at least one tag. Tags are case-sensitive.
Allow Self and Allow Others
These decide whether the Receiver reacts to Senders on your own avatar (Allow Self), to other people's Senders (Allow Others), or both.
Local Only
It only works in your own VRChat. It doesn't work on other people's screens. In return, it doesn't count toward your Performance Rank.

The Senders VRChat adds automatically have the tags listed on the Built-In Contact Tags page (opens in a new tab). The ones you'll use most are Head, Torso (body), Hand (either hand), HandL and HandR (left and right hand), Finger (any finger) and Foot. Put these tags on a Receiver, and it also reacts to the hands and fingers of other people's avatars.

You can adjust the position and size of the automatic Senders in the Colliders section of the VRC Avatar Descriptor. These Senders don't count toward your Performance Rank.

Choosing a Receiver Type

What the Receiver puts into the Animator parameter is chosen with Receiver Type.

Receiver TypeParameter typeValueGood for
ConstantBool (Float or Int also work)true (1) the whole time something is touching. false (0) when it leavesChanging your expression or blushing only while touched
OnEnterBool (Float or Int also work)true (1) for only one frame at the moment of touch. Goes back on the next frameStarting something once when touched (playing a sound, playing an animation once)
ProximityFloat only0 to 1. Gets closer to 1 nearer the center of the ReceiverChanging something more the closer it gets (how strong a blush is, how much the eyes narrow)

The Proximity value is how close the surface of the Sender is to the center of the Receiver. It goes just above 0 when the Sender touches the Receiver's edge, and reaches 1 when the Sender reaches the center. When several Senders are inside, you get the value of the closest one.

OnEnter lets you set Min Velocity. With it, the Receiver only reacts when something enters faster than a certain speed. You can use it for things like "don't react to a light touch, only to a pat".

Build a gimmick that blushes when your head is patted

Put a Receiver above your head so your cheeks turn redder the closer a hand or finger gets. Set the Receiver Type to Proximity, and use Motion Time to turn the closeness into the strength of the blush. How to build the Animator is explained in #8 Building a menu toggle, and Motion Time in #9 Switching in steps and changing gradually.

1. Place the Receiver

  1. In the Hierarchy, expand your avatar and find the head bone (Head) inside the Armature. The names and order differ by avatar (e.g. Armature → Hips → Spine → Chest → Neck → Head).
  2. Right-click Head and choose Create Empty. Name the new GameObject HeadPat. You make it a child of the head bone so the Receiver follows your head when it moves.
  3. With HeadPat selected, press Add Component at the bottom of the Inspector, type VRC Contact Receiver and add it.
  4. Set Shape Type to Sphere and Radius to about 0.1 to 0.15. A sphere outline appears in the Scene view. Raise the Y of Position a little so the sphere covers the top of your head. The values depend on your avatar's size, so decide while looking at the Scene view.
  5. Check Allow Others. If you also want it to react when you pat yourself, check Allow Self too. Leave Local Only unchecked (the reason is explained below in "Does everyone see the same thing?").
  6. Press Add under Collision Tags and choose Hand from the list. Press it again and add Finger too. If you type them in, match upper and lower case exactly.
  7. Set Receiver Type to Proximity and type HeadPat in Parameter.
  1. Make the sphere big enough to cover the top of your head.
  2. Reacts to other people's hands. To react to your own hands too, check Allow Self as well.
  3. These are the tags on the Senders VRChat automatically adds to hands and fingers.
  4. Puts a Float that gets closer to 1 the nearer something is into a parameter called HeadPat.

2. Make the blush clip

  1. Right-click in the Project window and choose Create → Animation to make a clip called Blush. See #7 Making animations for how to record.
  2. In the Animation window, switch the clip to Blush and press the record button. Move the time bar to 0 and set the face mesh's blush BlendShape (e.g. cheek_blush) to 0.
  3. Move the time bar to 1:00 (1 second) and set the blush BlendShape to 100. Stop recording. With Motion Time, the start of the clip is 0 and the end is 1, so if HeadPat is 0.5, the blush is 50.
  4. Don't write anything other than the blush BlendShape.

3. Make a layer in FX

  1. Open your Animator Controller in the Animator window, choose Float from + in the Parameters tab, and name it HeadPat. Match the name you typed in the Receiver exactly, including upper and lower case.
  2. Don't add HeadPat to Expression Parameters. The Receiver sets the value, so you don't need to change it from the menu.
  3. Press + in the Layers tab to make a HeadPat layer, press the gear and set Weight to 1.
  4. Drag the Blush clip from the Project window into the Animator window. You get one state, and it turns orange (the default).
  5. Select that state, check Parameter next to Motion Time in the Inspector and choose HeadPat. Set Write Defaults to match your other layers. You don't need any transitions.

To use Constant instead of Proximity, make the parameter a Bool, make two states with a blush 0 clip and a blush 100 clip, and transition between them on HeadPat true and false. Set Transition Duration to about 0.3 and the blush fades in slowly.

4. Connect it and test

  1. Connect your Animator Controller to FX with MA Merge Animator (#8 Building a menu toggle). The HeadPat GameObject is inside your avatar, so it's included in the upload as is.
  2. Press Play in Unity, make an empty GameObject in the Hierarchy, add a VRC Contact Sender to it, and put Hand in its Collision Tags. Move it close to your head in the Scene view and check whether the HeadPat value changes under Parameters in the Animator window.
  3. Testing in Unity can behave differently depending on your tools and versions. The most reliable test is to upload and ask a friend to pat your head. You can also check Allow Self for a while and test with your own hand (uncheck it again afterward).

PhysBone parameters (reacting when grabbed)

VRC Phys Bone, which makes hair and tails sway, can also pass values to the Animator. Type a name (e.g. Tail) in Parameter under the PhysBone's Options, and you can use the following parameters (VRChat's PhysBones page (opens in a new tab)).

ParameterTypeWhat it holds
Tail_IsGrabbedBooltrue while someone is grabbing it
Tail_IsPosedBooltrue while it's held in place in the shape it was left in after a grab (posed)
Tail_AngleFloat0 to 1. How far it has bent from its original direction. 1 when it points the opposite way (180°)
Tail_StretchFloat0 to 1. How far it has stretched. 1 at its longest (Max Stretch)
Tail_SquishFloat0 to 1. How far it has squished. 1 at its shortest (Max Squish)

Look surprised when your tail is grabbed

  1. In the Hierarchy, select the GameObject with your tail's PhysBone. Open Options on VRC Phys Bone in the Inspector and type Tail in Parameter.
  2. In Grab & Pose on the same PhysBone, check that Allow Grabbing is set so it can be grabbed. If it can't be grabbed, IsGrabbed never becomes true.
  3. Add Tail_IsGrabbed as a Bool to the Animator's Parameters.
  4. Make a Tail Face layer and set its Weight to 1. Make two states: put a clip that writes no expression (Face_None) in Idle, and the surprised face clip in Grabbed. Make Idle the default.
  5. Set the condition of the transition from Idle to Grabbed to Tail_IsGrabbed is true, and the one going back to false. On both, uncheck Has Exit Time and set Transition Duration to about 0.1.
  6. Put the Tail Face layer below your hand-gesture expression layers. When layers write the same face BlendShapes, the lower one wins, so the surprised face takes priority over gestures while your tail is grabbed.

How to make expression clips, and why the lower layer uses "a clip that writes no expression", are explained in #10 Changing expressions with hand gestures.

You can also allow grabbing and posing for only yourself or only other people. Also, depending on the other person's settings, they may not be able to grab it.

Does everyone see the same thing?

Parameters you change from the menu are set to Synced in Expression Parameters and sent to other people. Contact and PhysBone parameters work differently.

VRChat's documentation says a Receiver's Parameter doesn't need to be in the synced list in Expression Parameters (VRChat's Contacts page (opens in a new tab)). PhysBone parameters are the same: the PhysBone runs both in your VRChat and in other people's VRChat, and each one calculates the value. For Contacts that aren't Local Only too, think of it as each viewer's VRChat checking your avatar's Contacts.

So normally, other people see roughly the same reaction. However, hand positions arrive over the network with a small delay, so the moment of the reaction and its strength can be slightly different for each viewer. If you build something that counts with OnEnter, the count may not match between people.

Where the parameter comes fromOther people's screensExpression Parameters
Contact Receiver (not Local Only)Checked in each person's VRChat, looks roughly the sameNot needed
Contact Receiver (Local Only)Doesn't work. The reaction is only on your screenNot needed
PhysBone ParameterCalculated in each person's VRChat, looks roughly the sameNot needed
Parameters you change from the menu (for comparison)If Synced, sent to others and looks the sameAdd it

Use Local Only for reactions you don't need to show others (like a sound only you hear). Don't use it for reactions you want to show, like a blush.

If you also add a parameter with the same name to Expression Parameters and sync it, it becomes unclear on other people's screens whether the value sent to them or the value their own VRChat got from the Contact is used. Don't add it at first. If you really want everyone to see exactly the same value, there's a way to copy a Local Only Receiver's value into a synced parameter with VRC Avatar Parameter Driver, but that's for advanced users. See also #9 Switching in steps and changing gradually for how syncing works.

Cost and limits

Contact Senders and Receivers are counted under "Contacts" in the Performance Rank. On PC, up to 8 is Excellent, up to 16 is Good, up to 24 is Medium, and up to 32 is Poor (according to VRChat's Performance Ranks page, updated October 21, 2026). Local Only Contacts aren't counted.

On Quest and mobile, the limits are much lower. If you go over the Very Poor limit, all of your avatar's Contacts and PhysBones are removed. Touch gimmicks may not work for people on Quest or mobile.

How ranks are decided and the table of limits are explained in #4 Performance ranks and optimization, and how Quest and mobile handle them in #5 Quest and mobile support.

Common problems and fixes

Nothing happens when touched

What happens
Your cheeks don't turn red even when someone else pats your head.
Cause
One of these: the spelling or case of Collision Tags is different (hand vs. Hand, for example), Allow Others is off, the name or type of the Receiver's Parameter doesn't match the Animator parameter, or the layer's Weight is 0.
Fix
Check them in order from the top. If you press Play in Unity and watch whether the value changes under Parameters in the Animator, you can tell whether the Receiver or the Animator is the problem.

It reacts to your own hands too

What happens
Your cheeks turn red when you touch your own hair or move your hands near your head.
Cause
Allow Self is checked on the Receiver.
Fix
Uncheck Allow Self. It will then only react to other people's hands.

It reacts for an instant and stops

What happens
Something happens the moment you're touched, but it doesn't last while the touch continues.
Cause
The Receiver Type is OnEnter. OnEnter only sets the value for the one frame something enters.
Fix
If you want it to last while touched, use Constant (Bool) or Proximity (Float).

The blush stays on

What happens
Your cheeks stay red even after the hand leaves.
Cause
Write Defaults is OFF, and there's no clip that sets the blush back to 0, or no transition back to the original state.
Fix
With Constant, make a transition on false back to a blush 0 state. With Proximity and Motion Time, set a blush 0 key at the start of the clip.

Other people can't see the reaction

What happens
Your cheeks turn red on your screen, but people say they can't see it.
Cause
Local Only is checked on the Receiver. Or the viewer is on Quest or mobile and your Contacts have been removed.
Fix
Uncheck Local Only. For people on Quest or mobile, review the rank limits.

Nothing changes when your tail is grabbed

What happens
Your expression doesn't change when someone grabs your tail.
Cause
The PhysBone's Parameter is empty, or the Animator parameter name isn't Tail_IsGrabbed. Or Allow Grabbing is set so it can't be grabbed. It may also be above your expression layers and getting overwritten.
Fix
Match the Parameter name and the Animator parameter name (including case). Put the Tail Face layer below your expression layers.

How to check a gimmick that doesn't work is collected in #12 When a gimmick doesn't work.

Things to watch out for

  • Put the Receiver as a child of the bone you want to react. If you put it directly under the avatar, the Receiver won't follow your head when it moves.
  • If you make the Receiver too big, it reacts when something just passes nearby. Decide the size while looking at the sphere in the Scene view.
  • A Sender and a Receiver react if they share even one tag. If you add both Hand and Finger, it reacts to both palms and fingertips.
  • Contacts count toward your rank. Don't stack several Receivers in the same place. Reuse one Receiver's value in several layers instead.
  • If a reaction expression clashes with your hand-gesture expressions, decide which one wins with the layer order.

Summary

  • With Contacts, when a Sender enters a Receiver, the Receiver puts a value into an Animator parameter.
  • Use Constant for while touched, OnEnter for the moment of touch, and Proximity to change things by how close it is.
  • Both sides need the same Collision Tags, matching case exactly. Allow Self and Allow Others decide whose touch it reacts to.
  • Type a name in a PhysBone's Parameter, and you can tell it's being grabbed from _IsGrabbed and others.
  • Contact and PhysBone parameters usually look roughly the same to other people even without syncing. Local Only is only on your screen.
