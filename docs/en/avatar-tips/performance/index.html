Avatar Tips #4

Performance ranks and optimization

You uploaded your avatar and it was Very Poor. You added an outfit and the rank went down. This tip explains how the performance rank is decided, how your avatar looks to other people, and how to raise the rank (make the avatar lighter).

Published November 3, 2026 · This is about the PC version of VRChat. The limits come from VRChat's official page (updated October 21, 2026).

Read this when

  • You uploaded your avatar and its rank was Very Poor.
  • You added an outfit or accessories and the rank went down.
  • An event asked people to avoid Very Poor avatars.
  • Someone told you your avatar looks like something else to them.

What the performance rank is

The performance rank shows how heavy an avatar is (how much load it puts on a PC) in five levels. Each item, such as the number of polygons or materials, has its own limits.

Excellent
The best rank.
Good
A good rank. Aim for this one.
Medium
Despite the name, this is good enough. If you reach this, you rarely run into trouble.
Poor
A rank worth improving a little.
Very Poor
A heavy avatar. There is no upper limit, so it can be very heavy.

Your avatar's rank is the rank of its lowest item. Even if every other item is Excellent, a single Very Poor item makes the whole avatar Very Poor.

VRChat advises you to aim for Good, and if that is hard, Medium is enough.

The rank is only a guide based on counting the parts in your avatar. It doesn't look at how heavy the shaders or animations are. The rank isn't everything, but it helps you spot heavy avatars.

How other people see your avatar

In VRChat, viewers can choose the lowest rank they want to show (Minimum Displayed Performance Rank) in "Performance Options" at the top right of the "Safety" tab. On PC, the default is "Very Poor", so avatars of every rank show as they are. If a viewer sets it to "Medium" or "Poor", avatars below that rank look different, as shown below.

  1. On PC, the default is "Very Poor" (every avatar shows as it is). If you set it to "Medium", Poor and Very Poor avatars have parts removed or are replaced by a stand-in.
Item over the limitHow it looks
Triangles, texture memory, meshes, material slots, bonesThe avatar is replaced by a stand-in (Fallback)
PhysBones or ContactsAll PhysBones and Contacts are removed (hair and other parts stop swaying)
Particles, lights, audio and similarAll of those parts are removed

On mobile and Quest, only avatars up to "Medium" are shown from the start, and you can't choose to show Very Poor. This is covered in #5.

Busy events and groups may ask you to avoid Very Poor avatars. Even if you use a Very Poor avatar with close friends, it's good to have a light avatar ready for crowded places.

Things you have turned off are counted too. Outfits and accessories you switch from the menu still count toward meshes, materials and PhysBones while they are in the avatar, even when off.

There are other items too, such as size (Bounds Size), Constraints and the number of Animators. You can see every item on VRChat's Performance Ranks page (opens in a new tab).

Where to see the rank

You can see the rank in Unity before you upload, and in VRChat after you upload.

In Unity (before you upload)

  1. In Unity's top menu, open "VRChat SDK" > "Show Control Panel".
  2. In the "Builder" tab, select your avatar.
  3. The performance section shows the overall rank and the items that lower it.
  1. The rank of the whole avatar. It is the rank of the lowest item.
  2. The triangle count is in the Good range. Lowering it won't raise the overall rank.
  3. An item that lowers the rank. Reduce it to raise the rank.

In VRChat (after you upload)

  1. In the main menu, go to "Avatars", select your avatar, and press "Avatar Stats" at the bottom left.
  2. For someone else's avatar, open the Quick Menu, select that person, and press "Show Avatar Stats".
  3. The text color shows the rank of each item.

Why you should make your avatar lighter

Your avatar is drawn on other people's PCs too. A heavy avatar puts load on the PCs of everyone around you and lowers their frame rate (smoothness). In crowded places, this adds up.

In particular, many materials and meshes mean more draw commands (draw calls). This often matters more than the triangle count.

A lot of texture memory can use up the graphics card's memory (VRAM), which causes stutters or crashes.

How to make your avatar lighter

These are listed from most to least effective. Before you cut anything from the original avatar, try 1 and 2 first.

1. Add Avatar Optimizer (AAO)
A tool that makes your avatar lighter automatically when you upload, without changing how it looks. It removes unused parts, merges meshes, merges PhysBones and more. It doesn't change your original avatar. To install it, see "Optimize with AAO" below.
2. Delete outfits you don't use
Things you have turned off are counted too. For outfits and accessories you never switch to, don't just turn them off. Delete them from the avatar (it's safer to duplicate the avatar for uploading and delete them from the copy).
3. Merge meshes and materials
There are tools that merge meshes, and tools that combine textures into one (make an atlas) to reduce materials. Fewer materials and meshes mean fewer draw calls.
4. Reduce PhysBones
Remove PhysBones from parts that don't need to sway, or combine ones with the same settings into one. You can also reduce the number of bones they move.
5. Make textures smaller
Select a texture and lower "Max Size" in the Inspector (going from 2048 to 1024 cuts memory to about a quarter). Keep the size for textures that need fine detail, such as the eyes. "Use Crunch Compression" makes the download smaller, but it doesn't reduce texture memory.
6. Reduce polygons
There are tools that reduce polygons, but they easily break the look, so use them as a last resort. If your triangle count is in the Good range, fix the other items first.

Optimize with AAO

AAO doesn't change your original avatar. It makes the avatar lighter when you press Play and when you upload. If you no longer need it, just remove the component to go back.

  1. From the link on the AAO page (opens in a new tab), add anatawa12's repository to VCC or ALCOM (see #1 for how to add one).
  2. Add "AAO: Avatar Optimizer" on your project's management screen.
  3. In the Hierarchy, select the top of your avatar (the object with the VRC Avatar Descriptor).
  4. In the Inspector, use "Add Component" to find and add "AAO Trace And Optimize". The default settings are fine.
  5. Press Play and check that the look and gimmicks haven't changed, then upload. The Builder's rank may not show the numbers after AAO's optimization, so check "Avatar Stats" in VRChat after you upload.

#1 Adding tools and shaders

Common problems

Adding outfits made it Very Poor

What happens
You added a few outfits and made them switchable from the menu, and the avatar became Very Poor.
Cause
Outfits that are off are counted too, so the number of meshes, materials and PhysBones went up.
Fix
Delete outfits you don't switch to. Adding AAO's Trace And Optimize may also merge meshes and reduce the count.

Only the PhysBone items are Poor

What happens
Everything else is Good, but the PhysBones count or the bones they move make it Poor.
Cause
Each outfit comes with its own PhysBones for hair and clothes.
Fix
Remove PhysBones from parts that don't need to sway, or let AAO merge them.

Texture memory is too high

What happens
Texture Memory goes over 150 MB and the avatar becomes Very Poor.
Cause
There are many large textures, such as 4096.
Fix
Starting with textures on parts that don't stand out, lower Max Size to 2048 or 1024.

Other people see a different avatar

What happens
You can see your avatar, but others see a stand-in (Fallback) or a robot.
Cause
The viewer has set the displayed rank to Medium or similar, or is on mobile or Quest.
Fix
Raising your rank lets more people see your avatar. For people on mobile or Quest, prepare a version of your avatar that supports them (#5).

Things to watch out for

  • Before you cut anything, export your avatar's folder or back up your project. Some cuts can't be undone.
  • Before cutting things directly, try tools like AAO that don't change the original.
  • The rank is a guide. Medium is enough. You don't need to ruin a look you like just to raise the rank.
  • VRChat may change the rank limits. Check VRChat's official page for the latest numbers.

Summary

  • Your avatar's rank is decided by its lowest item.
  • Outfits you have turned off are counted too. Delete things you don't use.
  • First add AAO's Trace And Optimize, then review materials, PhysBones and textures.
  • Aim for Good, and if that is hard, Medium is enough.
