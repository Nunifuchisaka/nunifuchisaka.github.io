Avatar Tips #2

How to upload

This is the whole flow, from importing an avatar you bought into Unity to uploading it to VRChat. With recreated views of the screens, it explains the order to import shaders, how to read the VRChat SDK Builder, what to watch out for when updating, and common stumbling points.

Published November 3, 2026 · This covers VRChat SDK 3 (Avatars). It's up to date as of November 2026, and the screens may change when the SDK is updated.

Read this when

  • You bought an avatar, but you don't know how to use it in VRChat.
  • Red or yellow messages appear in the Builder, and you don't know what to do.
  • You want to upload an edited avatar again, or keep it as a separate avatar.
  • You uploaded an avatar, but you can't find it in VRChat.

The steps to uploading

This starts from the point where you've installed Unity and VCC (or ALCOM) and created a project for avatars. If you haven't yet, read #1 Installing VCC, ALCOM and Unity first.

What you need to upload

A VRChat account
Log in with the account you created on the VRChat website. If you play with a Steam or Meta account, link it to a VRChat account first.
"New User" rank or higher
Only accounts with a rank (Trust Rank) of "New User" or higher can upload. While you're a "Visitor", you can only use the test that only you can see (Build & Test). When you become able to upload, you'll get an email.
The avatar's terms of use
Each avatar and outfit has its own rules on how you may use it. In particular, check the download page or the included terms to see whether you may make it public (Public).

Importing shaders and the avatar

The avatar's instructions (README) list what to import first (shaders and tools) and in what order. Read them first.

  1. Import the shaders (lilToon, Poiyomi and so on) and tools (Modular Avatar and so on) listed in the instructions before the avatar. If they can be installed with VCC or ALCOM, install them from the project's manage screen. If they come as a .unitypackage, import them the same way as in the next step.
  2. Drag the avatar's .unitypackage into Unity's Project window. You can also choose it from Assets → Import Package → Custom Package....
  3. When the Import Unity Package window appears, just click "Import".
  4. When the import finishes, check the Console window for red errors.

If you import the avatar first, it turns pink because its shader is missing. In most cases, importing the shader afterward fixes it.

Placing it in the scene

  1. In the Project window, open the avatar's folder. Find the Prefab named after the avatar (the blue box icon).
  2. Drag the Prefab into the Hierarchy to place it in the scene. Some avatars come with a ready-to-use scene, so you just open it.
  3. Select the avatar in the Hierarchy and check that the Inspector has a "VRC Avatar Descriptor". Avatars sold in shops almost always have it set up already.
  4. Keep only the avatar you're uploading in the scene so you don't get confused. To hide ones you don't need, select them and uncheck the box at the top left.

Logging in to the VRChat SDK

  1. Click VRChat SDK → Show Control Panel in the menu at the top. The VRChat SDK window opens.
  2. On the "Authentication" tab, enter your VRChat user name (or email address) and password, and log in. If you use two-factor authentication, enter the code too.
  3. Open the "Builder" tab, and the avatar you placed in the scene appears.

Checking in the Builder and uploading

The Builder tab shows the avatar's name, its visibility setting, the check results (Validations), and the upload button.

  1. The name shown in your VRChat avatar list. You can change it later.
  2. Private means only you can use it. With Public, other people can switch into the avatar too, for example from avatar displays in worlds. Avatars sold in shops often forbid Public in their terms, so normally choose Private.
  3. Choose the kinds of warnings that apply, such as a lot of exposed skin or scary content. Viewers can then hide it in their settings.
  4. The image shown in the list. Capture how the scene looks right now, or choose an image file.
  5. Which devices you're building for. For the PC version only, choose Windows. To be seen on Quest or phones too, also build for those devices (there are requirements).
  6. A checkbox confirming that the information you entered is correct and that you have the right to upload it. You can't upload without checking it.
  7. Click it to start the build. When the build is done, it's sent to VRChat. You can't click it while there's a red error in Validations below.
  1. Enter a name and choose a Visibility. If you're not sure, choose Private.
  2. Choose any Content Warnings that apply, and capture a Thumbnail.
  3. Go through the messages in Validations at the bottom, starting from the top (see the next figure).
  4. Choose the devices to build for (Platforms), check the rights checkbox, and click "Build and Publish".
  5. Wait for the build and upload to finish. If the avatar is large, it takes a few minutes.

If you want to try it before uploading, use "Build & Test". It shows up in your VRChat avatar list as a test avatar that only you can wear. You can use it even with the "Visitor" rank.

How to read Validations (the check results)

Below the Builder are the results of the SDK's checks on your avatar. There are three kinds of messages.

ColorMeaningWhat to do
Red (error)A problem that stops you from uploadingYou can't click "Build and Publish" until it's fixed. Always fix it.
Yellow (warning)You can upload, but something needs attentionMost of the time, you can upload as it is. Read what it says, and fix what you can.
Blue or white (info)Information such as the performance rankJust read it. Nothing to do.
  1. If there's an "Auto Fix" button, clicking it makes the SDK fix the problem for you. Before you click, read the message to see what will change.
  2. Click "Select" to select the object causing the message in the Hierarchy. That shows you where to fix it.
  3. A rough guide to how heavy the avatar is. If the rank is low, some people may not see your avatar, depending on their settings. #4 Performance ranks and optimization explains this in detail.

Updating, or keeping it as a separate avatar

Once you upload, a component called "Pipeline Manager" is added to the avatar's top object, and a Blueprint ID (an ID that starts with avtr_) is written into it. This ID tells which avatar on VRChat it is.

If you upload with the same Blueprint ID, the previous avatar is overwritten. When you fix your edits and upload again, that's fine.

To keep your edited version as a separate avatar, click "Detach" in the Pipeline Manager to remove the ID, then upload. It gets a new ID.

  1. The ID of the avatar on VRChat that your upload goes to. The avatar with this ID is overwritten.
  2. Removes the ID. If you upload after this, it goes in as a new avatar.
  3. Used to create a new ID, or to upload to a specific ID. You normally won't need it.

If you duplicate the avatar in the Hierarchy (Ctrl + D), the Blueprint ID is copied too. If you upload the copy as it is, the original avatar is overwritten. Detach the copy first.

Checking in VRChat

  1. Open VRChat, go to the avatar list in the menu, and open the section with the avatars you uploaded.
  2. Select the avatar you uploaded and switch into it. If it isn't there, reload the list or restart VRChat.
  3. In front of a mirror, try everything: expressions, hand gestures, swaying parts, and things you control from the menu.
  4. How others see you also depends on ranks and Safety settings. If you're worried, ask a friend to take a look.

You can also see the list of avatars you uploaded on the "Content Manager" tab of the VRChat SDK in Unity.

Common problems

The VRChat SDK menu or the Builder doesn't appear

What happens
There's no VRChat SDK in the menu at the top. Or the Builder doesn't appear when you open the Control Panel.
How to fix it
Check the Console for red errors (compile errors). An error from a tool you imported can stop the SDK from working. Also check that your Unity version is the one VRChat specifies.

You can't upload (rank)

What happens
You can log in, but it says you can't upload.
How to fix it
Check that your account's rank is "New User" or higher. While you're a "Visitor", you can try the avatar by yourself with Build & Test. Your rank goes up as you play VRChat.

"Build and Publish" can't be clicked

What happens
The button is grayed out and can't be clicked.
How to fix it
Check that there are no red errors in Validations, and that you checked the rights checkbox. If clicking it does nothing, restart Unity.

The avatar turns pink

What happens
In Unity or VRChat, the avatar or its clothes turn solid pink.
How to fix it
The shader it uses isn't installed. Install the shaders listed in the instructions. If that doesn't fix it, select the material and choose the shader again.

A different avatar got overwritten

What happens
You uploaded a new avatar, and it replaced a previous one.
How to fix it
You uploaded with the same Blueprint ID, for example after duplicating. Detach in the Pipeline Manager and upload again. Upload the overwritten avatar again from its original project.

You uploaded it, but can't find it

What happens
The upload finished, but the avatar doesn't appear in your VRChat avatar list.
How to fix it
Check that the account you logged in to the SDK with is the same one you play VRChat with. Also check whether it appears in the SDK's Content Manager, and whether there are errors in the Console. If the SDK is old, update it with VCC or ALCOM.

Things to watch out for

  • Follow the terms of use for avatars and outfits. Whether you may make it Public, how far you may edit it, and whether you may share it with others are different for each model.
  • A Public avatar can be worn by other people too, for example from avatar displays in worlds. Keep avatars that are just for you Private.
  • A Fallback is the look shown instead to people who can't see your avatar because of ranks or Safety settings. You can set it from your avatar list in VRChat. Only light avatars can be set as a Fallback.
  • Back up your project before big edits, just to be safe.

Summary

  • Import shaders and tools first, then the avatar's .unitypackage.
  • Log in to the VRChat SDK, fix the red errors in the Builder's Validations, and then click Build and Publish.
  • If you're not sure, choose Private. Check the terms of use before making it Public.
  • Uploading with the same Blueprint ID overwrites the avatar. To make a separate avatar, Detach first.
