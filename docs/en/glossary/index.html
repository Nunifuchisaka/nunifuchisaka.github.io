VRChat life and culture

Words you'll hear inside VRChat and in conversations between people who play it. They aren't editing terms, but they come up when you make avatars and gimmicks too.

Kaihen (avatar editing)

Customizing an avatar you bought: putting outfits on it, changing its colors or shape, and so on.

You open an avatar bought on BOOTH or elsewhere in Unity, add outfits and gimmicks or change material colors, then upload it to VRChat. Japanese players call this kaihen. This site's Avatar Tips explain how to do it, one topic at a time.

Learn more #1 Installing VCC, ALCOM and Unity, #2 How to upload

Related terms Sotai (base avatar), BOOTH, Unity Hub

Sotai (base avatar)

The original avatar, before any outfits or accessories are added.

Used for an avatar as bought, with nothing edited. Outfits are often made for a specific base avatar and sold as "for (avatar name)".

Learn more #3 Dressing your avatar

Related terms Kaihen (avatar editing), Setup Outfit

World

A place in VRChat where people gather and hang out. Anyone can make and publish one.

Like avatars, worlds are made in Unity with the VRChat SDK and uploaded. Each world offers different things: mirrors, games, event venues, and more. Even in the same world, people are split up into separate instances.

Related terms Instance, Mirror, Portal

Instance

One copy of a world. You only meet the people in the same instance.

A world can have many copies open at once, each built the same way. Each one is an instance. Whoever opens it chooses who can join: Public (anyone), Friends+ (friends of anyone inside), Friends (only the opener's friends), Invite+ and Invite (only invited people), Group (members of a group), and so on.

Related terms World, Friend, Join

Friend

Someone you've added in VRChat. You can see where they are and go to meet them.

Send someone a friend request, and once they accept, you're friends. You can see which instance your friends are in, and visit them if the instance allows it (Join).

Related terms Join, Status (Join Me, Ask Me, and more), Instance

Join

Going straight to the instance a friend is in.

Pick a friend in the menu and press "Join" to move to their instance. Depending on the instance type and their status, you may not be able to get in. In that case you can ask for an invite (Request Invite).

Related terms Friend, Status (Join Me, Ask Me, and more), Instance

Status (Join Me, Ask Me, and more)

A colored marker that tells others whether you want visitors right now.

Join Me (blue) means "anyone come over", Online (green) is the normal state, Ask Me (orange) means "ask before you come", and Busy (red, formerly Do Not Disturb) means "please leave me alone for now". When you're on Ask Me or Busy, even friends can't Join you directly.

Related terms Friend, Join

Trust Rank

Your account's level, based on how much you've played and done in VRChat.

It goes up in the order Visitor, New User, User, Known User, Trusted User. Once you reach New User, you can upload avatars and worlds. It's also used in Safety settings to decide whose avatars and shaders you show.

Learn more #2 How to upload

Related terms Safety, VRChat+ (VRC+)

VRChat+ (VRC+)

VRChat's paid monthly subscription.

It comes with perks such as a badge next to your name, a custom profile picture, and more avatar favorite slots. You don't need it just to play.

Related terms Trust Rank

Desktop mode

Playing on a PC screen with keyboard and mouse, without a VR headset.

It's an easier way to start than VR. You can't make hand shapes, so gimmicks driven by hand signs don't work the same way as in VR. If you also let them run from the menu, everyone can use them.

Learn more #10 Changing expressions with hand gestures

Related terms Hand signs (GestureLeft, GestureRight), Expressions Menu (Action Menu), Full-body tracking (FBT)

Full-body tracking (FBT)

Wearing trackers on your hips and feet so your whole body's movement shows on your avatar.

With only a headset and controllers, VRChat tracks your head and hands, and guesses how your hips and legs move. Adding trackers to your hips and both feet (VIVE Trackers, body-worn sensors, and so on) lets sitting, crossing your legs, or lying down show up as they are. Your avatar's Humanoid setup needs to be correct.

Related terms Humanoid, Desktop mode

Mirror

A mirror placed in a world, where you can check how your avatar looks and moves.

Mirrors show you and other people's avatars. People often gather in front of a mirror to chat, which Japanese players call "mirror-mae" (in front of the mirror). Mirrors are heavy to draw, so many worlds let you switch between high quality (HQ) and low quality (LQ).

Related terms World

Portal

A doorway you walk through to move to another world or instance.

Choose a world in the menu and press "Drop Portal" to place a portal in front of you. People nearby can go through it with you. A portal disappears after a while.

Related terms World, Instance

Block and mute

Mute silences someone's voice. Block makes you and them invisible to each other.

Mute stops you from hearing that person's voice. Block stops both their avatar and voice from reaching you, and they can't see you either. They're features you should feel free to use whenever something bothers you.

Related terms Safety, Vision jacking (shikai jack)

Vision jacking (shikai jack)

A gimmick that uses a shader to cover other people's whole screen with color or images. Usually seen as harassment.

A shader that covers the whole screen can darken, recolor, or fill the view of people nearby. It's sometimes used as an effect at events, but using it on strangers without permission is harassment and can get you reported. If it happens to you, turning off that person's shaders in Safety, or blocking them, stops it.

Related terms Shader, Safety, Block and mute

Mute players (mugon-zei)

People who don't use a mic and talk with gestures and hand signs instead.

Some people play without talking, because they're somewhere they can't speak up, they'd rather not use their voice, and so on. They get their feelings across by nodding, waving, hand signs, expression gimmicks, or the chatbox that shows text over their head. If someone doesn't answer when you talk to them, they may be a mute player.

Related terms Hand signs (GestureLeft, GestureRight), Voice changer

Voice changer

A tool that changes your voice to a different pitch or quality when you talk.

People use one to match their voice to their avatar's look. There are software voice changers and hardware ones. Plenty of people talk in their own voice, and it's entirely up to you.

Related terms Mute players (mugon-zei)

Headpats (nadenade)

Patting someone's avatar on the head with your hand. A friendly greeting.

Some people add gimmicks (using Contacts) that move the ears, change the expression, or play a sound when a hand comes near the head. Some people don't like sudden headpats, so read the mood first.

Learn more #11 Gimmicks that react to touch

Related terms Contact (Sender and Receiver)

Osato ("sugar")

A romantic relationship between two people inside VRChat.

Japanese players say things like "we became osato" or call their partner their "osato-san". Often the two have never met in real life, and the relationship exists only in VRChat. When it ends, that's called oshio ("salt").

Related terms Friend

VR sleeping

Falling asleep in VRChat with your headset still on.

A way to play where you lie down in a world and sleep alongside friends. There are many dark, quiet worlds made for sleeping. Even without full-body tracking, you can lie down using things like a world's beds (seats that put you in a lying pose).

Related terms World, Full-body tracking (FBT)

Meetups (shukai)

A gathering held in a set world at a set time.

There are all kinds: meetups for people who share a hobby, tech talks, dance or music nights, and more. They're often announced on community-made event calendars or on X (formerly Twitter).

Related terms World, Instance

Beginner guiding

Showing someone new to VRChat the controls, how to play, and good worlds to visit.

A guide goes around with the newcomer and shows them how to use the menu, how to make friends, Safety settings, worlds worth visiting, and so on. There are also meetups where you can get a guided tour. If you've just started, see this site's Getting Started with VRChat too.

Related terms Safety, Friend, Meetups (shukai)

Software and tools

The software and tools you use to edit an avatar and upload it.

VCC (VRChat Creator Companion)

VRChat's official app for creating projects and adding tools and shaders to them (Windows only).

Creating a project in VCC gives you a Unity project with the VRChat SDK installed. For tools like Modular Avatar, you add the distributor's repository to VCC, then add the tool to your project. It doesn't run on Mac or Linux, so use ALCOM there.

Learn more #1 Installing VCC, ALCOM and Unity, #1 Installing VCC, ALCOM and Unity

Related terms ALCOM, Unity Hub, VRChat SDK

ALCOM

A community-made app that does what VCC does, and runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux.

You can use it the same way as VCC: create projects, add tools and shaders. Unlike VCC, it also works on Mac and Linux.

Learn more #1 Installing VCC, ALCOM and Unity, #1 Installing VCC, ALCOM and Unity

Related terms VCC (VRChat Creator Companion), Unity Hub

Unity Hub

Unity's official app for installing Unity and opening projects.

VRChat uses a specific version of Unity (2022.3.22f1 as of November 2026). Install that version through Unity Hub, and don't upgrade to newer ones.

Learn more #1 Installing VCC, ALCOM and Unity

Related terms VCC (VRChat Creator Companion), ALCOM, Project

VRChat SDK

VRChat's official toolkit, used inside Unity, for making and uploading avatars.

In a project with the VRChat SDK, a "VRChat SDK" menu appears at the top of Unity. From there you upload with the Builder and check your uploaded avatars in the Content Manager. Projects created with VCC or ALCOM have it from the start.

Learn more #1 Installing VCC, ALCOM and Unity, #2 How to upload

Related terms Builder, Content Manager, VCC (VRChat Creator Companion)

Avatars 3.0

VRChat's current avatar system. Gimmicks are built with the Animator, Expression Parameters, and the Expressions Menu.

The name of the system for building avatars that comes with the VRChat SDK. Every avatar uploaded to VRChat today runs on it.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works

Related terms Animator, Expression Parameters, Expressions Menu (Action Menu)

Built-in Render Pipeline

One of Unity's rendering systems. VRChat avatars are drawn with it.

Unity has several rendering systems (render pipelines), and VRChat uses the Built-in Render Pipeline. Shaders made for URP or HDRP can't be used on VRChat avatars.

Learn more #1 Installing VCC, ALCOM and Unity

Related terms Shader, Render Queue

Modular Avatar (MA)

A tool that builds outfits and gimmicks into an avatar without rewriting the original files.

Put MA components on an outfit or gimmick, and when you press Play or upload, MA builds them in automatically, following those components. It doesn't rewrite the original avatar's files, so to take something off, you just delete it.

Learn more #3 Dressing your avatar, #3 Dressing your avatar, #8 Building a menu toggle

Related terms MA Merge Armature, MA Merge Animator, MA Menu Installer and MA Menu Item

AAO (Avatar Optimizer)

A tool that automatically makes an avatar lighter at upload time without changing how it looks.

Add AAO Trace And Optimize to the top of the avatar, and it removes unused parts, merges meshes, and so on, making it easier to raise your Performance Rank. It doesn't rewrite the original data.

Learn more #4 Performance ranks and optimization

Related terms Performance Rank, Modular Avatar (MA)

Gesture Manager

A tool for trying menus and hand signs in Unity's Play mode, just like in VRChat.

Place it in the scene and press Play, and you can open the Expressions Menu and choose hand signs. It also shows parameter values, so when a gimmick doesn't work, you can find where it's getting stuck.

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle, #12 When a gimmick doesn't work

Related terms Expressions Menu (Action Menu), Parameter, Hand signs (GestureLeft, GestureRight)

lilToon

An anime-style shader widely used for Japanese avatars and outfits.

Many avatars and outfits are made assuming lilToon. If it isn't in your project, materials turn solid pink, so install it before the avatar.

Learn more #2 How to upload, #5 Quest and mobile support

Related terms Shader, Material

unitypackage

A format for bundling and sharing Unity files (file extension .unitypackage).

Avatars and outfits bought on BOOTH and elsewhere often come in this format. With your project open, drag it into the Project window to import it.

Learn more #2 How to upload

Related terms Project window, BOOTH

BOOTH

An online marketplace run by pixiv, where many avatars, outfits, and gimmicks are sold.

Before buying, check the store page for the supported avatars, the shader it uses, whether there's a Quest version, and what you're allowed to do with it (the terms of use).

Learn more #3 Dressing your avatar, #5 Quest and mobile support

Related terms unitypackage

Unity windows and parts

The names of Unity's windows, and how the things you place in a scene work.

Project

The unit you work in with Unity. One folder holds the avatar's and tools' files.

You create it with VCC or ALCOM and open it in Unity. It's fine to use a separate project for each avatar. Back up the whole folder from time to time so you can go back if something breaks.

Learn more #1 Installing VCC, ALCOM and Unity

Related terms VCC (VRChat Creator Companion), Project window

Hierarchy

The Unity window that lists the things placed in the scene (objects) as parents and children.

When you place an avatar, its body, clothes, Armature, and so on line up under the avatar's top object. Drag items to change which is the parent and which is the child.

Learn more #2 How to upload

Related terms Object (GameObject), Inspector, Scene

Project window

The Unity window that lists the files in your project (clips, materials, prefabs, and so on).

Use Create in its right-click menu to make folders, animation clips, and Animator Controllers. The Hierarchy shows things placed in the scene; the Project window shows files.

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle

Related terms Hierarchy, Prefab

Inspector

The Unity window that shows the contents and settings of the selected object or file.

Select something in the Hierarchy or the Project window, and its settings appear here. You also add and set up components here.

Learn more #2 How to upload

Related terms Component, Hierarchy

Scene

The place where you put objects and work on them. Avatars are placed in a scene before uploading.

The Scene view lets you move things while you look at them. A project can contain many scene files.

Learn more #2 How to upload

Related terms Hierarchy, Prefab

Object (GameObject)

Each item listed in the Hierarchy. You give it a role by adding components.

Body meshes, clothes, bones, and empty objects for gimmicks are all objects. The checkbox left of the name (Is Active) shows or hides it.

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle

Related terms Component, Is Active, Hierarchy

Is Active

Whether an object is shown or hidden. The checkbox left of the name in the Inspector.

Uncheck it, and the object disappears and its components stop working. When you toggle something with an animation clip, you write this value as 1 (show) or 0 (hide).

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle, #7 Making animations

Related terms Object (GameObject), Animation clip (clip)

Component

A part you attach to an object. It decides what's drawn, how it moves, and other roles.

Add them with "Add Component" in the Inspector. Skinned Mesh Renderer, PhysBone, VRC Avatar Descriptor, and Modular Avatar's parts are all components.

Learn more #3 Dressing your avatar

Related terms Object (GameObject), Inspector

Prefab

An object saved as a single file, contents and all. You drag it into a scene to use it.

In the Project window it shows up with a blue box icon. Avatars and outfits are often distributed as prefabs: you drag them into the scene, or into the avatar you want to dress.

Learn more #2 How to upload, #3 Dressing your avatar

Related terms Scene, Project window

The avatar's body and looks

The parts that make up an avatar's shape, bones, and colors.

VRC Avatar Descriptor

The component on the top of an avatar that holds its VRChat settings.

Here you set the view position, mouth movement when talking, the controllers in the Playable Layers, and the Expressions Menu and Expression Parameters. The object with this component is treated as one avatar.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works, #2 How to upload

Related terms Playable Layers, Expressions Menu (Action Menu), Expression Parameters

Humanoid

A fixed layout of human-shaped bones. VRChat uses it to map body movement onto the avatar.

Bones such as the arms, legs, head, and fingers are treated as Humanoid, and VRChat moves them. That's why animation clips in FX can't move those bones.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works, #7 Making animations

Related terms Armature, Bone, FX

Armature

The object that holds the avatar's bones. Bones line up as parents and children below Hips.

Outfits have their own Armature with bones of the same names. Modular Avatar's Merge Armature merges the outfit's bones into the avatar's, so the clothes move with the body.

Learn more #3 Dressing your avatar

Related terms Bone, MA Merge Armature, Humanoid

Bone

A bone that moves a mesh. When a bone moves, the mesh tied to it bends.

Bones line up as parents and children inside the Armature. Hair and skirt bones are swayed with PhysBones. The number of bones is also one of the Performance Rank categories.

Learn more #3 Dressing your avatar, #4 Performance ranks and optimization

Related terms Armature, PhysBone, Skinned Mesh Renderer

Mesh

Shape data built from triangles (polygons).

The body, hair, clothes, and so on are each made of meshes. The more meshes and triangles, the heavier it is to draw.

Learn more #4 Performance ranks and optimization

Related terms Polygon, Skinned Mesh Renderer, Material

Skinned Mesh Renderer

The component that draws a mesh that bends with bones. Bodies and clothes have one.

It lists the mesh and materials used, and the BlendShape values. To adjust expressions or body shape, open BlendShapes on this component.

Learn more #10 Changing expressions with hand gestures, #3 Dressing your avatar

Related terms Mesh, BlendShape (shape key), Material

BlendShape (shape key)

A setting that gradually changes a mesh's shape. Used for expressions and body shape. Values go from 0 to 100.

Each avatar comes with its own: smile, blink, mouth shapes, chest size, and so on. Write their values in an animation clip and you can change expressions with hand signs or the menu.

Learn more #10 Changing expressions with hand gestures, #3 Dressing your avatar, #13 It looks different in Unity and VRChat

Related terms Skinned Mesh Renderer, Animation clip (clip), Hand signs (GestureLeft, GestureRight)

Material

Color and surface settings. It decides which shader and which textures to use.

One mesh can use several materials. The more materials, the heavier it is to draw, so the material count is also a Performance Rank category.

Learn more #4 Performance ranks and optimization, #14 Render Queue (draw order)

Related terms Shader, Texture, Render Queue

Shader

A program that calculates how light hits a surface and what color comes out. lilToon, for example.

Each material chooses a shader. If the shader an avatar uses isn't in the project, it shows up solid pink. Quest and phones can only use the VRChat/Mobile shaders.

Learn more #2 How to upload, #5 Quest and mobile support

Related terms Material, lilToon, Quest, Android, iOS

Texture

An image wrapped onto a mesh. It paints skin, clothing patterns, colors, and so on.

The material decides which textures to use and how. Lots of large images increase texture memory and lower your Performance Rank.

Learn more #4 Performance ranks and optimization

Related terms Material, Atlasing

Polygon

The triangles that make up a mesh. More means finer detail, but heavier.

Performance Rank counts them as triangles (Triangles). Removing clothes and accessories you don't use reduces them.

Learn more #4 Performance ranks and optimization

Related terms Mesh, Performance Rank

PhysBone

VRChat's component that makes hair, tails, and skirts sway. They can also be grabbed.

Attach one to a bone and it sways as the body moves. It can report through parameters whether it's grabbed and how far it's bent, so you can use it in gimmicks too. Many of them get heavy, and they're a Performance Rank category.

Learn more #11 Gimmicks that react to touch, #4 Performance ranks and optimization

Related terms Bone, Contact (Sender and Receiver)

Bounds

An invisible box that fits around a mesh. It's used for drawing order and for Performance Rank.

The drawing order of transparent things is decided by the distance from the camera to the center of this box. If the box is too large, the "size" category of Performance Rank gets worse.

Learn more #14 Render Queue (draw order), #4 Performance ranks and optimization

Related terms Render Queue, Transparent

Animator and animation

Animator terms you use when you build gimmicks yourself.

Gimmick

A mechanism that switches an avatar's look or movement from the menu or with hand signs.

Examples include showing and hiding accessories, changing expressions, changing color or size, and reacting to touch. You build them by combining the Animator, parameters, and the menu.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works, #8 Building a menu toggle

Related terms Animator, Parameter, Expressions Menu (Action Menu)

Animator

The system that looks at parameters and decides which animation plays right now.

The Animator component on the top of the avatar runs according to the contents of the controller. In Unity's Animator window, you build layers, states, and transitions.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works, #6 How the Animator works

Related terms Animator Controller, Layer, State

Animator Controller

A file holding the Animator's contents (layers, states, transitions, parameters). File extension .controller.

Create one by right-clicking in the Project window. In VRChat you use it by putting it in a Playable Layers slot (such as FX). Ones you make yourself can be added to the avatar with Modular Avatar's Merge Animator.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works, #8 Building a menu toggle

Related terms Animator, Playable Layers, MA Merge Animator

Playable Layers

The slots VRChat provides on an avatar for controllers. There are FX, Gesture, Action, and others.

You put a controller in each slot on the VRC Avatar Descriptor. Gimmicks that change the look go in FX. These are different from the layers inside a controller.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works

Related terms FX, VRC Avatar Descriptor, Layer

FX

One of the Playable Layers. The slot for gimmicks that change the look, such as toggles, expressions, and colors.

Most gimmicks are built in FX. Humanoid bones (arms, legs, and so on) don't move from FX.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works, #7 Making animations

Related terms Playable Layers, Humanoid

Layer

A tier inside a controller. You usually make one per gimmick.

Layers run from top to bottom, and when two move the same thing, the lower layer wins. A layer you add won't take effect until you set its Weight to 1.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works, #10 Changing expressions with hand gestures

Related terms Weight (layer weight), State, Animator Controller

Weight (layer weight)

How strongly a layer applies (0 to 1). Layers you add start at 0, so set it to 1.

Change it from the gear on the right of the layer. If it stays at 0, that layer writes nothing and the gimmick doesn't work.

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle, #12 When a gimmick doesn't work

Related terms Layer

State

A box representing the current situation, such as "the hat is showing". One state holds one clip.

In the Animator window it's shown as a rectangle. The orange state is the one the layer enters first (the Layer Default State).

Learn more #6 How the Animator works, #8 Building a menu toggle

Related terms Transition, Animation clip (clip), Any State

Any State

A box meaning "whichever state you're in". Arrows from it move you from anywhere when the condition is met.

Handy when you have many states. While the condition stays true, it can keep re-entering the same state, so watch the Can Transition To Self setting.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works, #9 Switching in steps and changing gradually

Related terms State, Transition

Transition

An arrow from one state to another. When its conditions (Conditions) are met, you move along the arrow.

Right-click a state and choose "Make Transition" to draw one. For on/off gimmicks you usually uncheck Has Exit Time and set Transition Duration to 0.

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle, #6 How the Animator works

Related terms Has Exit Time, Transition Duration, State

Has Exit Time

A transition setting. When checked, the current clip plays to a set time before moving on.

If pressing the menu doesn't switch things right away, check whether this is ticked. For on/off gimmicks you normally uncheck it.

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle, #6 How the Animator works

Related terms Transition, Transition Duration

Transition Duration

A transition setting. How long to blend from the previous state into the next.

Set it to 0 to switch instantly. Make it a little longer when you want expressions and the like to change smoothly.

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle, #10 Changing expressions with hand gestures

Related terms Transition, Has Exit Time

Parameter

A value the Animator looks at. It changes from the menu or hand signs, and is used in transition conditions and more.

Add them in the Animator's Parameters tab. To change one from VRChat's menu or send it to other people, also add it to Expression Parameters (or MA Parameters) with the same name and type.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works

Related terms Bool, Int, Float (parameter types), Expression Parameters, Synced (sync)

Bool, Int, Float (parameter types)

Kinds of parameters. Bool is on or off, Int is a whole number (0 to 255), Float is a decimal.

Use Bool for on/off, Int to pick one of several, and Float to change something gradually. When synced, a Bool uses 1 bit, and an Int or Float uses 8 bits.

Learn more #9 Switching in steps and changing gradually

Related terms Parameter, Synced (sync), Radial Puppet

Animation clip (clip)

A file that says which property of which object to set to which value. File extension .anim.

Make one in Unity's Animation window by pressing the record button and changing values. You use it by putting it in a state.

Learn more #7 Making animations, #7 Making animations

Related terms Keyframe, Paths and Missing!, State

Keyframe

A point in a clip that fixes a value at a certain time. Shown as a diamond in the Animation window.

Even a clip whose value never changes needs at least one keyframe. Set two or more at different times, and Unity smoothly fills in the values between them.

Learn more #7 Making animations

Related terms Animation clip (clip)

Paths and Missing!

A clip remembers what it moves by a path of names. If a name or parent changes, the link breaks and Missing! appears.

When yellow Missing! text shows up in the Animation window, the object's name or location has changed since you made the clip. Change the name back, or fix the clip's path.

Learn more #7 Making animations

Related terms Animation clip (clip), Hierarchy

Write Defaults

A state setting. When ON, values the clip doesn't write go back to their originals. Keep it all ON or all OFF across the avatar.

Mixing ON and OFF causes mismatches that look fine in Unity but not in VRChat: expressions don't reset, objects don't disappear, and so on. To be safe, make clips for both on and off so it works either way.

Learn more #13 It looks different in Unity and VRChat, #8 Building a menu toggle

Related terms State, Animation clip (clip)

Blend Tree

A system that blends several clips together according to a parameter's value.

You use it inside a state. With one state, you can change things gradually by value, or bundle many gimmicks together.

Learn more #6 How the Animator works, #9 Switching in steps and changing gradually

Related terms Motion Time, Bool, Int, Float (parameter types)

Motion Time

A state setting that uses a Float value (0 to 1) to choose which point of the clip plays.

Write different values at the clip's start and end, and you can change color or size gradually, as far as you turn the menu. Used together with Radial Puppet.

Learn more #9 Switching in steps and changing gradually

Related terms Radial Puppet, Bool, Int, Float (parameter types)

VRC Animator Tracking Control

A part on a state that switches blinking and mouth movement between VRChat and the Animator.

When an expression clip moves the eyes or mouth, it can clash with VRChat's blinking and lip movement and break the expression. Use it to stop blinking or the mouth only while the expression is showing.

Learn more #10 Changing expressions with hand gestures

Related terms Hand signs (GestureLeft, GestureRight), State

Parameters and menus

Terms for running gimmicks from the menu inside VRChat.

Expression Parameters

The list that tells VRChat which parameters the avatar uses. It also decides whether each is remembered and sent to others.

You use it by putting it on the VRC Avatar Descriptor. With Modular Avatar, write them in MA Parameters and they're added automatically at upload.

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle, #6 How the Animator works

Related terms Synced (sync), Saved, MA Parameters

Synced (sync)

A setting that sends a parameter's value to other people. You can sync up to 256 bits in total.

Without the check, the gimmick only changes for you, and other people don't see it change. A Bool uses 1 bit, and an Int or Float uses 8 bits.

Learn more #9 Switching in steps and changing gradually, #6 How the Animator works

Related terms Saved, Expression Parameters, Bool, Int, Float (parameter types)

Saved

A setting that remembers a parameter's value when you change worlds or switch avatars.

Without the check, the value goes back to its Default every time you change worlds.

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle

Related terms Synced (sync), Expression Parameters

Expressions Menu (Action Menu)

The file that sets the contents of the round menu you open in VRChat (the Action Menu). Pressing an item changes a parameter.

For each item you set the parameter and value it changes, and its type (Toggle, Radial Puppet, Sub Menu, and so on). With Modular Avatar, you can add items with MA Menu Item.

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle, #6 How the Animator works

Related terms Toggle (menu item), Radial Puppet, Sub Menu

Toggle (menu item)

A menu item that switches between on and off each time you press it.

Pressing it sets the parameter to a chosen value, and pressing it again returns it to the original value (0). Often used to show and hide accessories.

Learn more #8 Building a menu toggle

Related terms Expressions Menu (Action Menu), Bool, Int, Float (parameter types)

Radial Puppet

A menu item where you turn a circle to change a Float value between 0 and 1.

Used to change things like color strength or size gradually. On the Animator side, you receive it with Motion Time or a Blend Tree.

Learn more #9 Switching in steps and changing gradually

Related terms Motion Time, Expressions Menu (Action Menu)

Sub Menu

An item that opens another menu when pressed. Used to split things up when you have many items.

One menu can only hold up to 8 items. Use it when you have more than that, or want to group items by kind.

Learn more #9 Switching in steps and changing gradually

Related terms Expressions Menu (Action Menu)

Hand signs (GestureLeft, GestureRight)

Hand shapes you make with the controllers. VRChat turns them into numbers 0 to 7 and puts them in GestureLeft and GestureRight.

The numbers are fixed: 0 is neutral, 1 is a fist, 2 is an open hand, and so on. Use these numbers as transition conditions to change expressions by hand shape. You can read how hard you're squeezing with GestureLeftWeight and GestureRightWeight.

Learn more #10 Changing expressions with hand gestures, #10 Changing expressions with hand gestures

Related terms VRC Animator Tracking Control, BlendShape (shape key), Parameter

Contact (Sender and Receiver)

VRChat's parts for detecting touch. The Sender does the touching; the Receiver gets touched.

VRChat automatically puts Senders on hands and fingers. Place a Receiver on the head or elsewhere, and you can read being touched as a parameter, so you can make gimmicks that react to headpats.

Learn more #11 Gimmicks that react to touch, #11 Gimmicks that react to touch

Related terms Receiver Type, Collision Tags, PhysBone

Receiver Type

The kind of value a Receiver outputs: Constant (while touching), OnEnter (the moment of touch), Proximity (how close).

Choose Constant to change something only while touched, OnEnter to start something once when touched, and Proximity to change it more strongly the closer something gets.

Learn more #11 Gimmicks that react to touch

Related terms Contact (Sender and Receiver)

Collision Tags

Contact tags. A Sender and Receiver react only when they share at least one tag.

There are fixed names, like Hand for hands and Finger for fingers. Upper and lower case are distinguished, so match the spelling exactly.

Setup Outfit

Uploading, and how others see you

Builder

A tab in the VRChat SDK window. It holds the name and visibility settings, the check results, and the upload button.

Validations

Blueprint ID

The ID of an uploaded avatar (starting with avtr_). Uploading with the same ID overwrites it.

Pipeline Manager

The component on the top of an uploaded avatar that remembers its Blueprint ID.

Content Manager

A tab in the VRChat SDK window that lists the avatars you've uploaded.

Performance Rank

How heavy an avatar is, in 5 levels: Excellent, Good, Medium, Poor, Very Poor.

Each category (triangle count, material count, bone count, PhysBone count, and so on) gets its own rank, and the worst one becomes the avatar's rank. Depending on the viewer's settings, avatars with low ranks are shown as a stand-in instead.

Learn more #4 Performance ranks and optimization, #4 Performance ranks and optimization

Related terms Fallback, AAO (Avatar Optimizer), Polygon

Draw call

A command to draw something on screen. More materials and meshes mean more of them, and more load.

Merging materials and meshes reduces them. Tools such as AAO can merge them automatically.

Learn more #4 Performance ranks and optimization

Related terms Material, Mesh, Atlasing

Atlasing

Combining several textures into one image to reduce the number of materials.

Fewer materials means fewer draw calls and a lighter avatar. Dedicated tools can do the combining automatically.

Learn more #4 Performance ranks and optimization

Related terms Texture, Material, Draw call

Safety

VRChat's settings for how much of other people's avatars you show.

Each viewer decides what to show, based on rank, whether someone is a friend, and so on. Even if your avatar looks fine to you, other people's settings may show you as a stand-in, or with your gimmicks stopped.

Learn more #4 Performance ranks and optimization

Related terms Fallback, Performance Rank

Fallback

The stand-in shown to people who can't see your avatar because of rank or Safety settings.

It's either an avatar you set as your Fallback, or a robot or similar provided by VRChat. For people on Quest or phones, it's shown in place of avatars that only have a PC version.

Learn more #4 Performance ranks and optimization, #5 Quest and mobile support

Related terms Impostor, Safety, Performance Rank

Impostor

A blurry stand-in made of flat panels, generated automatically by VRChat.

It's shown, for example, when people on Quest or phones see an avatar that only has a PC version. If the viewer has impostors turned off, they see the Fallback instead.

Quest, Android, iOS

VRChat running on standalone VR headsets such as the Meta Quest, or on phones. It needs separate avatar data from the PC version.

On Quest and phones, you can only use the VRChat/Mobile shaders, and the rank rules are stricter. Upload a Quest version with the same Blueprint ID, and people on Quest and phones can see you too.

Render Queue

A number for the order materials are drawn in. Lower draws first. Up to 2500 is opaque; around 3000 is transparent.

When transparent parts vanish or hide behind other things, revisit this number. You can change it in the material's Inspector.

Transparent

A way of drawing where you can see through to what's behind. Drawing order depends on distance, so overlaps easily break front and back.

Used for lace, glasses lenses, blush, and so on. Opaque things are drawn first, then transparent ones from far to near. When they overlap wrongly, offset the Render Queue to fix it.
